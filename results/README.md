### 2.4 `table04_temporal_regressions.csv`, `.html` & `table04b_half_decade_trends.csv` (`Step 07`)
* **`table04_temporal_regressions.csv` & `.html`:** Publication-formatted `broom` and `modelsummary`/`gt` OLS regression tables testing `M1` (STM Counter-Visuality, 1996–2024, $N = 2,155$), `M2` (STM Digital Turn Surge, 2010–2024, $N = 1,588$), `M3` (CLIP Panoptic Surveillance, 1996–2024, $N = 13,426$), and `M4` (CLIP Digital + Bureaucracy, 2010–2024, $N = 9,594$).
* **`table04b_half_decade_trends.csv`:** Aggregates mean STM motif prevalence across six half-decade buckets (`1996-1999` through `2020-2024`, following Arnold & Tilton, 2023, Table 3.1, p. 71), documenting the jump in total counter-visuality from **16.75%** (1996–1999) and **22.55%** (2005–2009) to **28.80%** (2010–2014) and **30.78%** (2020–2024).

### 2.5 `table05_material_motif_correlations.csv` (`Step 08`)
Contains all 325 bivariate Pearson correlation tests ($r$ and $p$-values) between the 25 STM visual motifs and the 13 cross-modal covariates (4 Material Medium categories, 6 CLIP Visual Clusters, and 3 HCI Formal Color/Light metrics), sorted by $|\text{Pearson } r|$.

### 2.6 `qualitative_artwork_dossier.txt` (`Step 09`)
A 34.3 KB structured close-reading dossier containing the **Top 5 Exemplar Artworks** for each of the 5 counter-visual motif families (25 artworks total), selected via `stm::findThoughts()` and ranked by a **Joint CLIP–LLaVA Composite Z-Score**:
$$Z_{\text{joint}, i} = 0.5 \cdot Z(\theta_{i, k^*}) + 0.5 \cdot Z(\text{CLIP}_{i, m})$$

Each dossier entry provides the artwork's Joint Z-Score, STM $\theta$, CLIP Cosine Similarity, PCAP Item ID, Title, Artist, Exhibition Year, Material Medium & Taxonomy Class, MDOC Facility & Security Level, Omeka S Web URL, Artist Statement, and full LLaVA Iconographic Transcript.

#### Methodological Guide to Reading the Dossier & Topic Table:
1. **Multimodal Triangulation Across Prompt Batches (`Topic 17` vs. `Topic 01` & `11`):**
   Because `pcap_llava_interrogations.jsonl` accumulated transcripts across two prompt iterations ($N = 1,200$ initial 4-part `(a)–(d)` deductive prompt + $N = 974$ inductive 3-paragraph "Objects + Stuff" prompt), **Topic 17** (`sensory, disalienation, spatial, identification, grid, optical`) captures a classic **VLM Prompt-Echo / Negation-Blindness** phenomenon where LLaVA echoed negative checklists from the initial prompt (*"there are no security cameras, watchtowers..."*). Crucially, because `Step 09` pairs STM $\theta$ with **CLIP Cosine Similarity**, the joint ranking still surfaces genuine high-signal exemplars within Family 1—such as Dave Wells's ***Trapped*** (ID 23926, `CLIP Sim = 0.195`, a screaming man bound in barbed wire) and Frank (FLAJ)'s ***The Eye of Us*** (ID 44062, `CLIP Sim = 0.171`)—while positive surveillance descriptions cluster cleanly in **Topic 01** (`surveillance, security, camera, confinement, watchtower`) and **Topic 11** (`eye, close, head, expression`).
2. **Lexical Anchor Polysemy & Joint Recovery (`Topic 21` & `Topic 16`):**
   In automated anchor matching (`Step 06`), **Topic 21** matched *Forensic Numbers & Barcodes* via `sign` and `letter`, surfacing both literal protest signage/letters—such as Terry Ulrich's ***Simple Reform*** (ID 58118, `CLIP Sim = 0.219`, a white sign with red letters reading *"Educate not encarcerate"*) and Robert T. Hinds's framed letter ***Walk With Me*** (ID 34958)—alongside 3D wooden craft. Similarly, while **Topic 16** matched *Telephonic/Screen Separation* on the word `glass` (grouping *memento mori* skull/glass paintings such as Clemente P. Pena's ***Tiempo Muerto [Dead Time]***, ID 26028), the joint CLIP `score_digital_mediation` ranking successfully surfaces Jeremy Lutze's ***JP6 Computer Box*** (ID 57590, `CLIP Sim = 0.216`), a 3D paper-and-glue sculptural replica of a prison computer monitor and keyboard.
